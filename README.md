# Cash, debt relief and a hospitality approach to immigration reform

Aaron Michael Weis · Oneiron Holdings LLC · Evidence review 1.0.0 · 18 November 2026

## What we learned, in everyday words

Imagine your family gets extra money each month. You can use some of it to buy groceries. If an old bill goes away, that helps with a different problem. You still need a safe place to sleep tonight.

In one U.S. study, households of adults offered $1,000 a month spent about $310 more per month on average than households of adults offered $50. Researchers could tell the groups spent different amounts. They could not tell with confidence whether the first group ended up owning more after subtracting its debts. [U.S. trial, Tables 4 and 15](https://www.alexbartik.com/papers/ORUS_Consumption_Sep2026.pdf)

A city called Rotterdam found old bills it could no longer collect. It crossed them off its books. It had already counted much of the money as unlikely to come back. Crossing off an old bill clears it from the books. It does not put that amount of money in a family's pocket or in a fund for housing. [Leiden program report, p. 23](https://www.universiteitleiden.nl/binaries/content/assets/rechtsgeleerdheid/fiscaal-en-economische-vakken/economie/bijstandsschulden-gemeente-rotterdam/eindrapport---bijstandsschulden-bij-de-gemeente-rotterdam.pdf#page=23)

It also matters what happens to families who miss out. In a Philippine program, researchers found that young children in families left out were more likely to be short for their age in villages where other families received money. A local pilot should check food prices and listen to neighbors as well as people who receive help. [Filmer and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1162/rest_a_01061)

## What this means for the proposal

Here, “Singularity UBI” refers to a proposed basic income financed by prospective AI-sector sponsors. “Jubilee” refers to the proposed cancellation of selected debts. The immigration proposal asks whether families could remain free in the community while receiving voluntary help during proceedings involving U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE).

The proposed Cook County, Illinois pilot would serve people with pending immigration proceedings, permission to live in the community and a need for accommodation. It would let families choose housing support, help finding a lawyer, regular cash and relief from selected private debts. Freedom to decline help, family unity and humane treatment are goals. The studies here concern other programs and populations. The evidence leaves the combined pilot's effect on family safety, detention, public costs and the national economy unknown. Wall removal would need its own evidence on authority, engineering, site conditions and cost. Corporate funding would require an actual commitment. This edition reports none from OpenAI or Anthropic.

The planning example covers 1,000 adults in 500 households, including 50 adults proposed for private-debt relief. The number of dependent children is unknown. For scale, the proposed cash benefit alone would cost **$12 million a year** if 1,000 adults each received $1,000 every month. Five full years at that enrollment would require **$60 million for cash payments**. The prior planning exercise put the five-year partial reference budget at **$167.705 million**, including those payments. Housing arrangements, service prices and financing still need verification before anyone can state a full cost. The study's $310 spending estimate cannot be multiplied by the pilot enrollment to predict a local economic gain.
